Future of AI - Large Conceptual Models

Large Conceptual Models (LCMs) are a new architecture in the field of natural language processing that differs from traditional language models (LLMs) in that they operate at the level of concepts rather than tokens (words). These concepts, represented by sentences, are encoded in an embedding space, which allows the model to reason at an abstract level, independent of language or modality (text, speech).

The operation of LCMs can be described in the following steps:
🔹 Sentence Segmentation: The input text is divided into sentences, which represent individual concepts.
🔹 Sentence Encoding: Each sentence is encoded using a SONAR model, which creates a vector representing its meaning.
🔹 Sequence Processing: The sequence of vectors representing the sentences is processed by the LCM, which generates a new sequence of embeddings.
🔹 Decoding: The generated sequence of embeddings is decoded back into text using a SONAR decoder.

LCMs offer several advantages over traditional LLMs, such as:
🔹 Improved coherence and logic of generated text: By operating at the concept level, LCMs can generate more coherent and logical texts, especially for long-form content.
🔹 Multilingualism: LCMs can be used to process text in multiple languages without having to learn each language separately.
🔹 Abstract reasoning: LCMs can reason at an abstract level, independent of language or modality.

LCMs are a promising architecture that could contribute to significant advances in the field of artificial intelligence.

This study by Apollo Research shows that with a prompt that sets a prompt with a very strong emphasis on following the model’s goal it will deliberately lie to the user when he or she has a different goal. It gives an ambiguous answer that on the surface looks ok, but what’s fascinating is the inner chain of thought how the model reason before giving the answer. Yes, you my say that the model still follows the initial prompt and that’s true. But that’s not the point. It “knows” that it lies to the current user. I had the opportunity to attend this seminar by Manjula Variyam last week! I thank her and Richardson IQ for pulling such a crucial topic in. The topic of IP is crucial not only for Entrepreneurs but anyone who deals with it on behalf of their organizations or as an individual need to understand the rules of engagement in a constantly changing tech landscape.

Manjula is an IP Attorney with a tech background. This gives her a vantage position in viewing Intellectual Property with dual yet appropriate perspectives - as a former Engineer who later entered the legal profession and has been practicing IP law for many years.

Sharing briefly some insights from her exhaustive presentation!
1. Patent: Most expensive and most valuable. Timing of disclosure is crucial and consider law of each country of application.
2. Trademark: Federal Trademark covers the entire US. Common Law covers local areas of use. Consider nuances of Text vs. Design mark. Intend to Use version could provide some preemptive protection.
3. Copyright: No specific type of Copyright for Software (on par with books) and still needing hard copy print out for the deposit copy! Need to consider changes of code, cloud versions, and use of open source. Creator of the work owns it – so companies need to make sure contracts cover this.
4. Trade Secret: Cyber-Security of assets typically used as proof a company tried to protect their trade secrets. Re-engineering is allowed in the US. Data (e.g. Proprietary LLM) cannot be Copyright protected but can be a treated as a Trade Secret.

Lastly, it is crucial for organizations to have an AI policy to guide their employees and suppliers on this topic (summary covered in the main post by Richardson IQ).

13 Web Development Trends

Table of Contents:
1, Progressive Web Apps (PWAs)
2, Single Page Applications (SPAs)
3, Accelerated Mobile Pages (AMP)
4, Responsive Design
5, AI and Chatbots
6, Motion UI
7, Serverless Architecture
8, API-first Development
9, Blockchain Technology
10, Internet of Things (IoT)
11, Cybersecurity
12, Dark Mode Standardization

1. Progressive Web Apps (PWAs):
The first of 13 web development trends is: PWAs combine the best of web and mobile apps, providing a seamless user experience with offline capabilities, fast loading times, and push notifications.

2. Single Page Applications (SPAs):
SPAs load a single HTML page and dynamically update content as the user interacts with the app, resulting in a faster and more responsive experience similar to desktop applications.

3. Accelerated Mobile Pages (AMP):
AMP is a Google-backed project aimed at speeding up content delivery on mobile devices. AMP pages load faster, improve SEO, and provide a better user experience on mobile.

4. Responsive Design:
Responsive design ensures websites work well on a variety of devices and screen sizes, providing an optimal user experience whether on a mobile phone, tablet, or desktop computer.

5. AI and Chatbots:
AI-driven chatbots provide instant customer support and can handle a wide range of tasks, from answering queries to guiding users through a website, improving engagement and satisfaction.

6. Motion UI:
Motion UI involves using animations and transitions to create engaging and interactive web experiences. It helps guide users, provide feedback, and enhance the visual appeal of a website.

7. Serverless Architecture:
Serverless architecture allows developers to build and run applications without managing servers. This reduces complexity and costs, and improves scalability and flexibility.

8. API-first Development:
API-first development focuses on designing robust APIs before building the actual application. This approach improves integration, scalability, and enables microservices architecture.

9. Blockchain Technology:
Blockchain technology is being explored for its potential in enhancing security, transparency, and efficiency in web development, particularly in areas like data protection and decentralized applications.

10. Internet of Things (IoT):
The integration of IoT in web development allows for creating applications that interact with smart devices, providing users with real-time data and automated control over their environments.

11. Cybersecurity:
With increasing cyber threats, robust cybersecurity measures are critical. This includes secure coding practices, regular updates, encryption, and multi-factor authentication.

12. Dark Mode Standardization:
Dark mode is becoming a standard feature in web design, providing users with a dark-themed interface option that reduces eye strain and saves battery life on OLED screens.
