Just had a piece published in Progressive Dairy – Canada magazine (Issue 4, April 2025, pg. 31), where Herman and I discuss how bulk tank milk (BTM) can be used as a practical surveillance tool for contagious mastitis pathogens like 𝘚𝘵𝘢𝘱𝘩. 𝘢𝘶𝘳𝘦𝘶𝘴, 𝘚𝘵𝘳𝘦𝘱. 𝘢𝘨𝘢𝘭𝘢𝘤𝘵𝘪𝘢𝘦, and 𝘔𝘺𝘤𝘰𝘱𝘭𝘢𝘴𝘮𝘢 𝘣𝘰𝘷𝘪𝘴. This was based on my presentation at the National Mastitis Council (NMC) 2025 meeting earlier this year where I presented part of my PhD research that focuses on the surveillance of infectious diseases.

Since mastitis control strategies vary depending on the specific pathogen, identifying the cause of mastitis in your herd is crucial for targeted prevention and treatment. Regular BTM screening helps catch infections early before they become widespread in herd, allowing producers to take timely, informed action.

Understanding the bacteria involved, implementing effective control measures and working closely with a herd veterinarian can improve herd health, milk quality and overall farm profitability.
